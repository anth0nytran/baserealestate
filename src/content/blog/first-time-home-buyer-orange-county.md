Most people buy their first home having never been taught how the process works. They learn it in real time, under deadline, with the largest cheque of their life on the table. That is a bad way to learn anything.

This guide is the walkthrough I would give you in a first consultation. It will not make you an expert. It should make the transaction stop feeling opaque.

## What does it actually cost to buy in Orange County?

More than the down payment, and the gap surprises people.

Orange County's median for existing detached single-family homes was **$1,470,000** in July 2026 (C.A.R.). Counting all property types, including condominiums and townhomes, Redfin put the August 2026 median at roughly **$1,200,000**. That $270,000 difference is worth understanding before you budget — condos and townhomes are a genuine entry path here, and they are what pulls the second figure down.

Beyond the purchase price, plan for:

- **Closing costs.** Escrow, title, lender fees, and recording. These are real money and they are due at close.
- **Property tax.** California's base rate under Proposition 13 is 1% of assessed value, plus local voter-approved assessments. Your assessed value resets to your purchase price.
- **Insurance.** Coastal proximity, fire risk zoning, and older construction all affect what you will pay, and in some coastal and canyon areas availability itself is a live issue. Get a quote before you are in contract, not after.
- **HOA dues**, if applicable — common in Irvine, Laguna Niguel, and much of the planned south county.
- **Mello-Roos**, if applicable. These are community facilities district assessments attached to certain newer developments, and they are additional to your property tax.
- **Reserves.** What is left after closing. A purchase that leaves you with nothing is a purchase that cannot absorb a water heater.

## What changed for buyers in 2025?

You now sign a written agreement with your agent before you make an offer, and it states what you owe them.

California Civil Code §1670.50, effective 1 January 2025, requires a written buyer-broker representation agreement executed no later than when you submit an offer. It must state the compensation, the services provided, when compensation is due, and the term — which cannot exceed 90 days for an individual buyer. Separately, national practice changes from August 2024 mean buyer-agent compensation is no longer published on the MLS, and you need a written agreement before touring a home.

**This does not necessarily mean you pay out of pocket.** Sellers frequently still cover some or all of that compensation as a concession negotiated in the purchase agreement. What changed is that it is negotiated and written down rather than assumed and invisible.

Read that agreement before you fall in love with a property. Ask specifically what happens if the seller contributes less than your agreed figure.

## What is the market like for a first purchase right now?

Better than it was, in the ways that matter to a first-time buyer.

- **Active listings: about 5,165** in late August 2026, a high for the year. More to choose from.
- **Median days on market: about 54**, up from roughly 40 a year earlier. You are far less likely to face a same-weekend decision.
- **Prices: up about 3.2%** year over year. Not a discount.

The practical effect is that the frantic conditions of 2021–2023 have eased. You can see a property twice. You can order an inspection and actually read it. You can negotiate a repair credit instead of waiving everything to win.

## Where do first-time buyers actually buy in Orange County?

Honestly: mostly not on the coast, and there is no point pretending otherwise.

Coastal south county medians run far above the county figure — Newport Beach was reported between $3.4 million and $4.47 million over the six months to August 2026 depending on what is counted, and Laguna Beach around $3.1 million. Those are not first-purchase numbers for most people.

Realistic entry paths include attached homes — condominiums and townhomes — in Costa Mesa, Irvine, Laguna Niguel, and Huntington Beach, and detached homes further inland. The all-types median of roughly $1.2 million reflects those transactions.

That is not a consolation prize. Buying an attached home in a location you actually want, with reserves intact, is a considerably better outcome than stretching for a detached house you cannot comfortably carry.

## The eight steps, in order

1. **Get pre-approved, not pre-qualified.** Pre-qualification is an estimate. Pre-approval means a lender has examined your documents. Sellers treat the two very differently.
2. **Sign your buyer-broker agreement**, having read it.
3. **Define your actual criteria.** Not a wishlist — the two or three things that are genuinely non-negotiable, and everything you would trade for them.
4. **Search, and see enough properties to calibrate.** The first five are education. Do not offer on the second.
5. **Make an offer, on terms as much as on price.** Contingencies, timeline, and credits are all negotiable and all matter.
6. **Inspect properly, and read the report.** On older coastal and canyon properties, expect findings. The inspection is your leverage.
7. **Negotiate repairs or credits.** This is frequently the largest number after the purchase price.
8. **Close.** Final walkthrough, signing, funding, recording, keys.

## The deadlines that cost people money

California purchase contracts run on contingency periods, and they are the part first-time buyers underestimate most.

Your contingencies — typically inspection, appraisal, and loan — give you a defined window to investigate and to withdraw with your deposit intact. Once a contingency is removed, that protection is gone. Removing one before you have the information it was protecting you against is the single most expensive avoidable mistake in a first purchase.

Two specific traps:

**The appraisal gap.** If the appraisal comes in below the contract price, your lender lends against the lower figure and the difference becomes your problem. Know before you offer what you would do if this happens.

**Removing contingencies to win.** In a competitive situation you may be encouraged to waive protections. Sometimes that is a considered risk. At a 54-day median days on market with inventory at a yearly high, it is far less often necessary than it was two years ago.

## What should you ask before you commit to an agent?

- What happens if the seller contributes less than my agreed compensation?
- Which comparable sales support this price, and can I see them?
- What did you find in the inspection report that concerns you?
- What would make you tell me not to buy this property?

That last one is the most informative question you can ask anyone in this business.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much do I need to buy a house in Orange County?

Beyond the down payment, budget for closing costs, property tax at California's 1% base rate plus local assessments, insurance, any HOA dues or Mello-Roos assessments, and post-closing reserves. The county median was $1,470,000 for existing detached single-family homes in July 2026, and roughly $1,200,000 across all property types in August 2026 — attached homes are the more common entry path.

### Do first-time buyers have to sign a contract with a real estate agent in California?

Yes. Since 1 January 2025, California Civil Code §1670.50 has required a written buyer-broker representation agreement executed no later than when you make an offer, stating the compensation, the services, when payment is due, and the term. For an individual buyer the term cannot exceed 90 days. National practice changes since August 2024 also require a written agreement before touring a home.

### Does the buyer pay the agent commission in California now?

The buyer is contractually responsible under that written agreement, but sellers frequently still cover some or all of it as a concession negotiated in the purchase contract. Many buyers still pay nothing out of pocket. Ask your agent directly what happens if the seller offers less than your agreed figure.

### What is Mello-Roos and will I have to pay it?

Mello-Roos is a community facilities district assessment used to fund infrastructure in certain developments, and it is charged in addition to your property tax. It is common in newer planned communities. Whether you pay it depends on the specific property, and it should be identified before you make an offer.

### Is it a good time for a first-time buyer in Orange County?

Conditions are more favourable than in 2024. Active listings reached about 5,165 in late August 2026, a yearly high, and median days on market rose to roughly 54 from about 40 a year earlier — meaning more choice, more time to inspect, and more room to negotiate credits. Prices are still up about 3.2% year over year.

### What is the biggest mistake first-time buyers make?

Removing a contingency before they have the information it was protecting them against — most often waiving inspection or appraisal protection to win a competitive situation. In the current market, with inventory at a yearly high and homes taking about 54 days to sell, that is much less often necessary than it was two years ago.
