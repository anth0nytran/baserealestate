Every homeowner in Orange County has typed their address into a valuation tool. Most have typed it into three, got three different numbers, and come away less certain than when they started.

That is a reasonable reaction, because the numbers genuinely are different and none of them are wrong exactly. They are answering a narrower question than the one you asked.

## What is my home actually worth?

Your home is worth what a qualified buyer will pay for it, in the current market, given its specific condition and location. Every valuation method is an attempt to estimate that number, and they differ in what evidence they are allowed to look at.

An automated estimate looks at public records and recent nearby sales. That is genuinely useful in a tract subdivision where three hundred homes share four floor plans. It is much weaker on the coast, where the property next door may be worth a million dollars more for reasons no database records.

## Why automated estimates struggle on the Orange County coast

Five things move coastal value substantially, and an algorithm can see none of them.

**View corridor.** Not "ocean view" as a yes/no field, but what you actually see, from which rooms, and whether anything can be built to block it. Two homes on the same street with the same square footage can differ enormously on this alone.

**Coastal permit history.** Much of the coastline sits within the Coastal Zone. Whether past work was permitted, and what a future buyer will be allowed to do, is a material value factor and it lives in municipal and Coastal Commission records rather than in a sales database.

**Deck, setback, and structure legality.** Older coastal properties frequently carry improvements that were never permitted. A beautiful deck that cannot be legally rebuilt is a liability disclosed at inspection, and it changes the number.

**Orientation and exposure.** Sun, prevailing wind, marine layer, and street noise vary block by block near the water. Buyers price these differences even when they do not articulate them.

**Condition, honestly assessed.** A database sees the last sale price and the square footage. It does not see the roof, the plumbing, or the fact that the kitchen was renovated in 2006 rather than last year.

None of this means automated estimates are worthless. It means they are an orientation, and on the coast they are a wide one.

## What does a comparative market analysis actually examine?

A comparative market analysis, or CMA, is what an agent prepares when you ask what your home is worth. Done properly it is not a number pulled from software — it is an argument built from evidence.

The work is:

1. **Identify genuinely comparable closed sales.** Similar size, similar condition, similar location characteristics, sold recently enough to reflect the current market. On the coast this is the hard part, because true comparables are scarce.
2. **Adjust for the differences.** No two properties are identical, so each comparable is adjusted up or down for what it has that yours does not, and vice versa.
3. **Check what is currently competing.** Closed sales tell you where the market has been. Active listings tell you what a buyer will be choosing between when they see yours.
4. **Look at what failed.** Expired and withdrawn listings are the most under-used evidence in pricing. They tell you where the ceiling actually is, which is usually more decisive than where the floor is.
5. **Account for time.** How long comparable homes took to sell, and in which price bands, determines whether your target price is realistic on your timeline.

A CMA is normally provided free, including the one I prepare. A licensed appraisal is a different instrument, generally costs several hundred dollars, and is usually ordered by a lender during a transaction rather than by an owner deciding whether to sell.

## Why is there a gap between asking prices and sale prices?

Because they measure different things, and right now the gap is unusually wide.

In Newport Beach over the six months to August 2026, the difference between the median asking price on active listings and the median sold price reached about **$2.4 million** — the widest recorded.

That does not mean values dropped by $2.4 million. It means the homes that are selling and the homes that are sitting have become two different populations. Well-priced properties transact; aspirationally-priced ones accumulate on the active list and pull the asking median up behind them.

The practical consequence: **do not price against active listings.** The active median is inflated by homes that have already failed to sell at that number. Price against closed sales, which is the only evidence of what someone actually paid.

## What is the market doing right now?

Across Orange County as of the most recent published data:

- Median for existing detached single-family homes: **$1,470,000** (C.A.R., July 2026), up about 3.2% year over year
- Median across all property types: **≈$1,200,000** (Redfin, August 2026)
- Median days on market: **≈54 days**, up from about 40 a year earlier
- Active listings: **≈5,165** in late August 2026, a high for the year

For a coastal property, treat the county figures as background only. Coastal south county medians run well above them — Newport Beach was reported between $3.4 million and $4.47 million over the six months to August, depending on whether attached homes are counted.

## The two-week rule

Whatever number you land on, understand what your first two weeks buy you.

When a listing goes live, it is shown to every buyer who has been waiting and watching in that price band. That audience is the largest and the most motivated it will ever be. If the price tests a hopeful number, those two weeks are spent proving it does not work, and the reduction that follows is presented to a smaller and more sceptical pool.

With active inventory at a yearly high, that audience has more alternatives than it did a year ago. The cost of testing a number has gone up.

## What should you do before you list?

Get a real analysis, and get it early — six to twelve months before you intend to move if you can.

That timing lets you do the things that actually change the number: address the deferred maintenance that shows up at inspection, resolve permit issues before a buyer's agent finds them, and prepare the property properly rather than in a rush. Those are decisions with lead times, and they are worth more than any pricing cleverness at the end.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How accurate are Zillow and Redfin estimates in Orange County?

They are reasonable orientation in uniform tract neighbourhoods and considerably less reliable on the coast. Automated models work from public records and nearby sales, and cannot account for view corridor, coastal permit history, unpermitted improvements, orientation, or condition. In Laguna Beach, Dana Point, and Newport Beach those factors routinely move value by six figures.

### How much does a home valuation cost in Orange County?

A comparative market analysis from an agent is normally free, including the one I prepare. A licensed appraisal typically costs several hundred dollars and is usually ordered by a lender during a transaction. For deciding whether and how to sell, the market analysis is the more useful instrument.

### Why do three different agents give me three different values?

Because a valuation is an argument from evidence, and agents may select different comparable sales, adjust them differently, or weight active competition differently. Ask each one to show you which closed sales they used and why. The reasoning matters more than the number, and an agent who cannot show the comparables is quoting a feeling.

### Should I price high and negotiate down?

Rarely, and it is more costly now than it used to be. Your first two weeks reach the largest and most motivated audience your listing will ever get. Spending them testing a high number means the inevitable reduction is presented to a smaller, more sceptical pool. With Orange County active listings at a yearly high, buyers have more alternatives than they did a year ago.

### What is the difference between asking price and sale price right now?

In Newport Beach over the six months to August 2026, the median asking price on active listings exceeded the median sold price by roughly $2.4 million — the widest gap recorded. The homes selling and the homes sitting have become different populations, which is why pricing against active listings rather than closed sales is a costly mistake.

### How long does it take to sell a home in Orange County?

About 54 days at the median as of August 2026, up from roughly 40 days a year earlier. On the coast, timing varies sharply by price band: Newport Beach homes near $4.5 million have been selling in under three weeks, while properties above $7 million have been sitting for months.
