I get asked this every week, and the honest answer is that "is it a good time to buy" is not really a market question. It is a question about your rate, your horizon, your down payment, and what waiting actually costs you.

What I can do is tell you precisely what has changed, so you are deciding against facts rather than against headlines.

## What has actually changed for buyers?

Three things, and they all moved in the same direction.

**Inventory is at a yearly high.** Active listings reached about **5,165** in late August 2026. For most of the last five years, Orange County buyers competed over scarce supply. That is the condition that has genuinely eased.

**Homes are taking longer to sell.** Median days on market is around **54 days**, up from roughly **40** a year earlier. A fourteen-day increase is a larger signal than the price change, and it is where a slowing market shows up first.

**Prices have not fallen.** The California Association of REALTORS® reported a July 2026 median of **$1,470,000** for existing detached single-family homes, against $1,425,000 in July 2025 — up about **3.2%**.

Put together: you have more to choose from, more time to decide, and more room to negotiate on terms. You do not have a discount.

## Does more inventory mean prices are about to drop?

Not by itself, and this is the inference people most often get wrong.

Rising inventory in Orange County is being driven substantially by homes that are not selling at their asking price rather than by a flood of new distressed supply. In Newport Beach, the gap between the median asking price on active listings and the median sold price reached about **$2.4 million** over the six months to August 2026 — the widest recorded.

That gap describes a sorting, not a collapse. Realistically-priced homes are transacting; aspirationally-priced ones are accumulating. The active list grows, the sold median holds, and a headline that reads "inventory surges" describes both facts without distinguishing them.

## What does this mean on the coast specifically?

Coastal south county is behaving less uniformly than the county aggregate suggests.

Over the six months to August 2026 in Newport Beach, homes around **$4.5 million** were moving in under three weeks, while homes **above $7 million** were sitting for months and in some cases not selling at all. A single citywide days-on-market figure averages those into a number that describes neither.

For a buyer, that stratification is opportunity. The bands where inventory is accumulating are the bands where sellers are most willing to move — on price, and more often on terms.

## What can you actually negotiate right now?

In a market like this one, terms are frequently worth more than the price concession you did not get. Specifically:

- **Repair credits.** On older coastal properties, inspections surface real findings — roofs, decks, plumbing, unpermitted work. A credit against those is often the largest number on the table after the purchase price itself.
- **Closing timeline.** Sellers who will not move on price will often move on a date that suits you, which has real value if you are coordinating a sale of your own.
- **Contingency periods.** More time to inspect and to secure financing is worth having, and it costs a seller nothing but patience.
- **What stays with the property.** Rarely decisive, occasionally worth five figures.

None of these appear in a median price, and all of them are more available at 54 days on market than they were at 40.

## Should you wait?

Here is the calculation I would walk you through, and it has nothing to do with predicting the market.

**What does waiting cost you?** If you are renting, twelve months of rent is the direct cost. Against that, weigh what you would gain: a larger down payment, a better rate if rates move, or simply more certainty about your own plans.

**What is your horizon?** If you expect to hold for seven to ten years, the entry month matters far less than people believe. If you may need to sell within three, timing matters enormously and the current transaction costs plus a 54-day median sale time deserve serious weight.

**What happens if you are wrong?** This is the question most people skip. If you buy now and prices soften, can you comfortably hold? If you wait and prices rise another 3%, does your target neighbourhood move out of reach? Whichever answer is more survivable is usually the right decision.

Prices are up year over year. Anyone who waited for a crash has, so far, paid more. That is not a prediction — it is what the record shows.

## What would make me tell you to wait?

I do tell people to wait, and these are the reasons:

- Your down payment would leave you with no reserve after closing.
- You are likely to move again within two or three years.
- Your income is about to change in a way you have not yet resolved.
- You have not been pre-approved, and you are shopping on an estimated budget rather than a real one.
- You are buying because you feel behind rather than because the property works.

None of those are market conditions. They are the reasons a purchase goes wrong regardless of what the market does.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is 2026 a good year to buy a house in Orange County?

It is a better year for buyers than 2024 was, for two measurable reasons: active listings reached about 5,165 in late August 2026, a yearly high, and median days on market rose to roughly 54 from about 40 a year earlier. That means more choice and more negotiating room. Prices are still up about 3.2% year over year, so it is not a discount.

### Are Orange County home prices going down in 2026?

Not at the median. The California Association of REALTORS® reported $1,470,000 for existing detached single-family homes in July 2026 against $1,425,000 in July 2025, an increase of about 3.2%. Active inventory is rising, but that is being driven substantially by homes not selling at their asking price rather than by falling sale prices.

### Will more inventory push Orange County prices down?

It has not so far. Rising active listings alongside a stable sold median indicates the market is sorting between realistically-priced homes, which are selling, and aspirationally-priced ones, which are accumulating. In Newport Beach the gap between median asking and median sold price reached roughly $2.4 million over the six months to August 2026, the widest recorded.

### What can I negotiate as a buyer in Orange County right now?

More than price. At a 54-day median days on market, sellers are frequently more flexible on repair credits, closing dates, contingency periods, and included items than on the headline number. On older coastal properties, the repair credit following inspection is often the largest negotiable figure after the purchase price.

### Should I wait for interest rates to drop before buying?

That depends on what waiting costs you and how long you plan to hold. If your horizon is seven to ten years, the entry month matters much less than people assume. If you may need to sell within three years, timing and transaction costs deserve real weight. Prices have risen about 3.2% over the past year, so waiting has carried a cost recently.

### Is it a buyer's market or a seller's market in Orange County?

Moving toward balance, without having arrived. Buyers have gained choice and time; sellers have retained pricing. Active listings at a yearly high and a 54-day median sale time are buyer-favourable conditions; a sold median still rising year over year is not.
