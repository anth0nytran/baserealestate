Most "market update" articles are a national headline with a local city name pasted on top. Orange County does not behave like the national market, and the coastal south county does not behave like Orange County as a whole.

This report uses only figures I can point you to a source for, and it tells you plainly when a number is my own calculation rather than a published statistic. Where two credible sources disagree — and on the headline number, they disagree by a lot — I show you both and explain why.

## Why do two sources report Orange County medians $270,000 apart?

Because they count different homes.

The California Association of REALTORS® reported a median of **$1,470,000** for July 2026. That figure counts *existing detached single-family homes only*. It excludes condominiums, townhomes, and new construction.

Redfin reported a median of roughly **$1,200,000** for August 2026. That figure counts *every property type*, condos and townhomes included. Because attached homes sell for materially less than detached ones in this county, including them pulls the median down.

Neither source is wrong. The gap is methodology, not disagreement about the market.

This matters more than it sounds. A buyer who reads "$1.2 million" and budgets for a detached house in Laguna Niguel is short by a quarter of a million dollars before they start. A seller who reads "$1.47 million" and prices a two-bedroom condo accordingly will sit on the market. The first question to ask about any median is *which homes are in it*.

## What is the Orange County median home price right now?

If you mean a detached single-family house, use **$1,470,000** (C.A.R., July 2026). If you mean housing generally, including condos and townhomes, use **roughly $1,200,000** (Redfin, August 2026).

For year-over-year context: C.A.R. put the July 2025 median at $1,425,000, so detached single-family prices are up about **3.2%** over twelve months. That is appreciation, but it is modest appreciation — well below the double-digit years many owners still anchor to.

## Is inventory rising or falling in Orange County?

Rising. Active listings reached roughly **5,165** in late August 2026 — a high for the year.

That is the single most useful number in this report, and it is the one that has actually changed. For most of the last five years Orange County buyers competed over scarce inventory. More listings means more choice, more time to think, and more willingness from sellers to negotiate on terms.

It does not mean prices are falling. It means the balance of pressure has moved.

## How long are Orange County homes taking to sell?

About **54 days** at the median, up from roughly **40 days** a year earlier (Redfin, August 2026).

A fourteen-day increase in median days on market is a bigger signal than a three percent move in price. Days on market is where a softening market shows up first — sellers hold their price and simply wait longer for it, and only later does the price itself adjust. If you are watching for a turn, watch this number, not the median.

## What does this mean if you are buying?

The market has handed buyers three things it was not handing them two years ago:

- **Choice.** More than five thousand active listings is enough that you can be selective about location and condition rather than taking what is available.
- **Time.** A 54-day median means you are far less likely to face a same-weekend decision on a seven-figure purchase.
- **Negotiating room on terms.** In a slower market, sellers who will not move on price will often move on the repair credit, the closing date, or the contingency timeline. In coastal south county, where inspection findings on older properties are common, the repair credit is frequently worth more than the price concession you did not get.

What it has not handed buyers is a discount. Prices are up year over year. Anyone waiting for a crash is, so far, waiting.

## What does this mean if you are selling?

Price to the evidence, not to last year.

The most expensive mistake in a slowing market is anchoring to a number a neighbour got in a faster one. Your first two weeks on market are the only two weeks you get with a fresh, motivated audience. A list price that tests a hopeful number burns those weeks, and the price reduction that follows arrives in front of a smaller, more sceptical pool of buyers.

With inventory at a yearly high, your listing is being compared against more alternatives than at any point this year. Condition and preparation are doing more work than they were in 2023.

## Does the coastal south county behave differently?

Yes, and county-wide figures obscure it.

Newport Beach, Laguna Beach, Dana Point, and Laguna Niguel are thin, differentiated markets. A canyon lot in Laguna and a harbour-adjacent property in Dana Point are not interchangeable, and neither is well described by a county median that includes Anaheim condos.

In practice this means three things for the coast:

1. **Comparable sales matter more than medians.** With fewer transactions per neighbourhood, a single unusual sale moves a local average dramatically. County-level data is context, not a pricing tool.
2. **The range is enormous.** A county median tells you almost nothing about what a specific street commands.
3. **Time on market runs longer at the top.** Higher price bands have fewer qualified buyers by definition, so the 54-day county median understates how long a distinctive coastal property can reasonably take.

If you are pricing or budgeting for a specific coastal property, the county median is a starting orientation and nothing more. The number that matters is what genuinely comparable homes on comparable streets actually closed at, and how long they took.

## How often is this report updated?

Monthly, against published sources. Every figure above carries the month it was observed, because Orange County conditions change month to month and a number without a date is a claim, not data.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the median home price in Orange County right now?

For existing detached single-family homes, $1,470,000 as of July 2026 according to the California Association of REALTORS®. For all property types including condos and townhomes, roughly $1,200,000 as of August 2026 according to Redfin. The two figures differ by about $270,000 because they count different homes, not because the sources disagree.

### Are Orange County home prices going up or down in 2026?

Up modestly. The California Association of REALTORS® reported a July 2026 median of $1,470,000 for existing single-family homes against $1,425,000 in July 2025, an increase of about 3.2% year over year. Prices are appreciating while the market itself slows, which is a normal combination.

### Is Orange County a buyer's or seller's market right now?

It is moving toward balance. Active listings reached about 5,165 in late August 2026, a high for the year, and median days on market rose to roughly 54 days from about 40 a year earlier. Buyers have meaningfully more choice and more time than they did in 2024, but prices have not fallen.

### How long does it take to sell a house in Orange County?

About 54 days at the median as of August 2026, up from roughly 40 days a year earlier. Higher price bands and distinctive coastal properties typically take longer, because the pool of qualified buyers narrows as price rises.

### Should I wait for prices to drop before buying in Orange County?

That is a personal calculation, not a market one, and it depends on your rate, your horizon, and what waiting costs you. Prices are up year over year, so waiting has not paid off recently. What has changed in buyers' favour is choice and negotiating leverage on terms, both of which are better now than they were two years ago.

### Why do Zillow, Redfin, and my agent all give different home values?

Because they use different data and different methods. Automated estimates work from public records and recent nearby sales, and cannot see condition, upgrades, view, orientation, or street-level differences. Those factors move coastal Orange County values by six figures. A comparative market analysis prepared by an agent who has walked the comparable homes accounts for what an algorithm cannot.
