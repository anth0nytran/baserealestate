If you have tried to find out what homes cost in Newport Beach, you have probably found three answers and no explanation of why they differ. This article is that explanation, plus the numbers themselves with the source and the window attached to each one.

The short version: **there is no single median price for a city.** There is a median for a particular set of homes, over a particular period, from a particular source. Change any of those three and the number changes, sometimes by more than a million dollars.

## Why do two reports on the same city disagree by $1,000,000?

Newport Beach is the clearest example I have seen.

Over the same six-month window ending August 2026, one report put the median at **$4,470,000** across 231 sales. Another put it at **$3,400,000** across 381 closings.

Both are defensible. The first counts detached single-family homes only. The second counts every closing, which in Newport Beach means a large volume of condominiums and attached properties on the peninsula and around the harbour. Adding roughly 150 mostly-attached sales to the pool pulls the median down by over a million dollars.

Neither figure is the "real" price of a Newport Beach home, because "a Newport Beach home" is not one thing.

A few things fall straight out of that table.

**The county median is useless on the coast.** The Orange County figure of $1,470,000 is below every coastal city median here. A buyer orienting off county data will be short by a factor of two or three in Newport Beach or Laguna Beach.

**The spread between coastal cities is larger than most people expect.** Laguna Niguel and Laguna Beach are about eleven minutes apart and differ by roughly $1.7 million at the median. They are not substitutes, and a search that treats them as interchangeable is not a real search.

**The windows are not comparable.** The Laguna Beach figure covers a different three months than the others cover six. I have left it in because it is the best sourced figure I have for that city, but it is not directly comparable to the rows above and below it, and I would rather show you that than quietly line up numbers that do not belong in the same column.

## What is actually selling in Newport Beach right now?

This is the more useful question, and the answer is more interesting than the median.

Two patterns stand out in the six months to August 2026:

**Price bands are behaving completely differently.** Homes around the $4.5 million mark have been moving in under three weeks. Homes at $7 million and above have been sitting for months, and some are not selling at all. A single citywide "days on market" figure averages those two realities into a number that describes neither.

**Asking prices and sale prices have diverged sharply.** The gap between the median asking price on active listings and the median sold price reached about **$2.4 million** — the widest recorded. That gap is not telling you the market crashed. It is telling you that *the homes that are selling are not the homes that are sitting.* Well-priced properties are transacting quickly; aspirationally-priced properties are accumulating on the active list and dragging the asking median upward.

If you are a seller, that second pattern is the one to take seriously. The active median is inflated by homes that are not selling. Pricing against it means joining them.

## How should you use these numbers?

Three rules, in order of how much money they save you.

1. **Check what the median counts before you use it.** Detached only, or all types? That single distinction was worth a million dollars in Newport Beach. Ask it every time.
2. **Never price a specific property off a city median.** Coastal south county markets are thin and highly differentiated. A canyon lot in Laguna, a harbour-adjacent property in Dana Point, and a hillside home in Laguna Niguel share a county and almost nothing else. What matters is what genuinely comparable homes on comparable streets closed at, and how long they took.
3. **Watch days on market and the ask-to-sold gap, not the median.** Medians move slowly and lag. Time on market and the divergence between asking and sold prices move first, and they tell you where the leverage is.

## The thing nobody tells you about coastal medians

A median tells you the middle of what sold. It does not tell you what is available, what is good, or what you can get.

In a market where the top of the range is sitting and the middle is moving in three weeks, the median is a statistic about *transactions*, not about *value*. Two identical-looking Laguna Beach properties can differ by a million dollars on view corridor, coastal permit history, and whether the deck is legally permitted. No median captures any of that.

This is why I start with the mechanics rather than with listings. If you understand what moves value on a specific street, you can evaluate a property yourself. If you only have a citywide median, you are guessing with a very large number.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the median home price in Newport Beach?

It depends on what is counted. Over the six months to August 2026, one source reported $4,470,000 across 231 detached single-family sales, while another reported $3,400,000 across all 381 closings including condominiums and attached homes. The roughly $1,070,000 difference comes entirely from whether attached properties are included.

### What is the median home price in Laguna Niguel?

Approximately $1,395,000, based on 387 closed sales over the six months to August 2026. That figure counts all closings rather than detached single-family homes only.

### How much more expensive is Laguna Beach than Laguna Niguel?

At the median, roughly $1.7 million. Laguna Beach was reported at about $3,100,000 for the three months to May 2026, against about $1,395,000 for Laguna Niguel over the six months to August 2026. Note that those two figures cover different periods, so treat the comparison as an order of magnitude rather than a precise gap.

### Why is the Orange County median so much lower than coastal city prices?

Because the county median includes the entire county, and most of Orange County's transaction volume happens inland at much lower prices. The county figure of $1,470,000 for July 2026 is below every coastal city median in this article. County-level data is the wrong tool for a coastal purchase.

### Are Newport Beach home prices falling?

The data does not show falling sale prices. It shows a widening gap between what sellers are asking and what buyers are paying — roughly $2.4 million at the median, the widest recorded. Homes priced near $4.5 million have been selling in under three weeks; homes above $7 million have been sitting. That is a market sorting itself by price realism, not a broad decline.

### Should I use Zillow or Redfin estimates for a coastal Orange County home?

Use them as orientation and nothing more. Automated valuations work from public records and nearby sales, and cannot see view corridor, coastal permit status, deck and setback legality, orientation, or condition — all of which move coastal values by six figures or more. In thin, highly differentiated markets like Laguna Beach and Dana Point, those blind spots matter more than anywhere else in the county.
